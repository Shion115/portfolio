Research Profile

I am a Computer Science and Engineering graduate interested in understanding how people interact with
AI-enabled systems and how interface design influences their decisions. My undergraduate thesis combines
web-based interface design, NLP, and a controlled user study to examine how different explanations affect
people’s understanding and use of AI outputs. I enjoy building interactive research prototypes and evaluating
them with users, and I hope to continue studying how AI system design shapes people’s choices, understanding,
and sense of control.

Undergraduate Thesis: Designing and Evaluating Explanation Interfaces for AI-Assisted
Medical Misinformation Detection
Human–Computer Interaction | NLP | Explainable AI | Human–AI Decision Making
• Designed and implemented four web-based explanation interfaces that communicate AI assessments,
  linguistic indicators, calibrated probabilities, uncertainty, evidence, and sources; built the end-to-end
  research prototype with Next.js and FastAPI.
• Conducted a controlled HCI study with 60 participants and 1,200 judgment trials. The most
  informative interface improved judgment accuracy from 73.7% to 90.0% and reduced over-reliance on
  incorrect AI advice from 77.8% to 37.8%.
• Developed the NLP backend using RoBERTa, calibrated uncertainty, and conformal selective
  prediction on a multi-source corpus of 17,611 samples. The selective system automatically handled
  91.56% of cases with macro-F1 0.9806, while referring uncertain cases for human verification.
